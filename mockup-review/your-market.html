Your Market

Under Insights. Rebuilt since the last round — now a decision page, not a table of ZIPs. One page, three parts.

Open Your Market

What it is

Your Market is the page that answers "where and when should we run the next campaign?" It used to be a big table of ZIP codes; now it's built to push you toward a decision. It reads market data — sales, rents, loan patterns — for your ZIPs and turns it into a few clear moves.

How it works. Three parts, top to bottom. First, a quick read on what changed this week (rates, local rules, your ZIPs). Second, three ready-made campaign ideas, each with a reason and a one-click way to start it. Third, a deep-dive module where you pick any ZIP and see everything about it. The whole page is meant to be read start to finish, and the two buttons that do something — "New list for this ZIP" and "Create this campaign" — carry what you're looking at straight into building a list or a campaign.

What this area should do for you

  • Tell you, in under a minute, which ZIP is the best place to campaign next — and why.
  • Turn a market observation into a started campaign without re-entering anything.
  • Give you the depth to back up a decision (or a listing pitch) when you want it, but not force it on you.
  • Replace the spreadsheet-style ZIP table with something that actually recommends a move.

Part 1 · This week

The strip across the top. Four quick items, each a fact plus a suggested move and a source link: where interest rates are, a change in Oakland's rent rules, what's happening in your ZIPs, and what's coming up (like a City Council meeting). For example: "30-year fixed at 6.18%, down 0.24 in four weeks — move 2023–24 buyers up the call list." It's redrawn weekly from public sources.

What we want to hear from you

  • Is a weekly "here's what changed" strip useful, or is it the kind of thing you'd skip past?
  • The "Act" and "Talk track" lines tell you what to do with each fact — helpful, or do they feel like they're telling you your job?
  • Is anything you watch every week missing from these four?

Part 2 · Where to campaign next

Three cards, each a ready-made campaign idea. Every card names a ZIP and area, gives the idea a title, says why it's worth doing, backs it with three facts, and suggests which piece to send. At the bottom is one button that starts it: Create this campaign (n), where n is how many owners it would reach. In the demo:

  • 94608 · Emeryville border — "2027 loan decisions." 38 owners with loans likely due next year, in a ZIP where 45% of sales drew 3+ offers. Suggested piece: Market update.
  • 94702 · West Berkeley — "Just sold nearby." 64 owners who live near a recent sale, in the ZIP with the most competitive sales. Suggested piece: Just Sold postcard.
  • 94609 · Temescal — "Long-time owners." 112 owners who've held 15+ years, in your busiest ZIP. Suggested piece: MVI letter.

Pressing Create this campaign opens the new-campaign screen with the list and the suggested piece already filled in. The cards refresh on Thursdays.

What we want to hear from you

  • Do these read like campaigns you'd actually run, or like a computer guessing? Would you trust the pick?
  • Is three the right number — enough to choose from, not so many it's noise?
  • The reason and three facts on each card: enough to decide, or would you need to dig into the ZIP first?
  • If you disagreed with a suggested piece (say it proposes a postcard but you'd send a letter), would you want to change it here or on the next screen?

Part 3 · ZIP detail

The full-width module at the bottom, and the only place to browse a single ZIP in depth. Pick a ZIP from the list (grouped by city, with sale counts) and the whole module updates in place. For the chosen ZIP you get: a plain-language summary; six key-number tiles (with "Print-safe" and "Thin sample" labels where they apply); trend charts for price per unit, sales by quarter, and days on market; a breakdown of how buildings sold (empty vs. occupied, cash vs. financed, how many offers); rents with a rough yield; an Opportunity section (owners ranked Hot / Warm / Watch, loans coming due, long-time owners); and the last few sales. Two buttons: New list for this ZIP and Create this campaign.

Note: some counts (Opportunity, Events) are placeholder numbers for now and are labeled "illustrative."

What we want to hear from you

  • When you want to know a ZIP cold — for a campaign or a listing pitch — is everything you'd want here?
  • Six key numbers plus three charts: the right depth, too much, or too little?
  • The "How buildings sell" breakdown (empty vs. occupied, cash vs. financed, offers) — is that something you'd use, or inside-baseball?
  • "New list for this ZIP" vs. "Create this campaign" — is the difference between those two buttons clear?
  • Would you trust the Opportunity rankings (Hot / Warm / Watch) if they were real, or would you want to see how they're figured?

Scenario: where to run the next campaign

Optional. Talk out loud as you go. The data is fixed, so you're pretending.

The loan-maturity campaign is winding down and you want to line up the next one. You've got fifteen minutes and no particular ZIP in mind. Start on Your Market.

  1. Catch up. Read the This week strip. Is there anything here that would change where you'd campaign? Say so out loud.
  2. Pick from the ideas. Look at the three "Where to campaign next" cards. Which one would you run, and why? Say whether you're convinced by the card alone, or whether you need more.
  3. Dig in. Take the ZIP from the card you picked and open it in the ZIP detail module. Does the deeper data back up the idea, or make you reconsider?
  4. Second-guess it. Now open a ZIP the cards didn't suggest. Would you have picked it over the recommended one? If the cards missed something you'd have caught, that's exactly what we want to hear.
  5. Start it. Press Create this campaign on the idea you chose (it won't really do anything in the demo). Say whether you'd expect the next screen to have the list and piece ready, or whether you'd want to set those yourself.
  6. Wrap up. Did this page actually help you decide, or would you still go to a spreadsheet or your gut? Be blunt — that's the whole test of this screen.
