Start Here

How to review the mockup and record your feedback. About 10 minutes to set up, once. Use a laptop or desktop, not your phone.

What you'll be doing

We'll walk through the new software one area at a time. For each area you'll open the mockup, turn on a screen recording, and click through it while saying out loud what you think — what's clear, what's confusing, what you'd do differently. We watch the recordings to find the rough spots. Nothing is final; blunt is useful.

The mockup is a clickable demo. Buttons take you from screen to screen, but nothing saves and the data is made-up sample data for buildings in Oakland. Greyed-out items in the left menu are later phases — skip them.

Install Loom

Loom records your screen and your voice together and gives you a link to share. It's free and installs in a couple of minutes.

  1. Go to loom.com and create a free account with your work email. Josh will invite you to the Prescott workspace so recordings land in one place — accept that invite if you see it.
  2. Download the desktop app from loom.com/download (Mac or Windows). Use the desktop app, not the browser extension — it's steadier for a longer recording.
  3. Open Loom and sign in. The first time, your computer asks it for permission to record the screen and use the microphone — say yes to both. On a Mac, if recording looks blocked, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording and turn Loom on.
  4. Do a 10-second test: start a recording, say one sentence, stop, play it back. If you can hear your voice and see your mouse move, you're set.

Record a review

Before you start. Open the area's guide below and read it (about 5 minutes). Open the mockup in its own browser window. Close Slack and email so nothing pops up mid-recording.

Start the recording. In Loom choose Screen + Camera (or Screen only if you'd rather not be on camera), pick the browser window with the mockup, check your microphone is on, and press Start recording.

While you click, talk. This is the whole point. Say what you're thinking before you click, and say what you expected after you see what happens. Silence is the only thing we can't use. Phrases that help most:

  • "Yes, I understand this — I know exactly what it's for."
  • "This doesn't make sense to me because…"
  • "I expected this to… but it…"
  • "I'd never do it this way. What I do today is…"
  • "This is missing…" / "I don't need this at all."
  • "I don't know what this word means."
  • "I'm stuck — I was looking for…"

Point with your mouse at whatever you're talking about. If you get stuck, say so and tell us what you were hunting for — that's useful, not a failure. Don't quietly figure it out and move on; the confusion is exactly what we're after.

Save and share it

When you stop, Loom opens the video's page and copies the share link automatically. Two quick steps:

  1. Rename it Area – Your name – Date — for example, Owners – Nolan – Nov 10.
  2. Paste the link into the PLS Review Google Drive folder (Josh will share it with you).

About the 5-minute limit. Loom's free plan caps each recording at 5 minutes. Most areas fit. If you run long, either Josh puts you on a paid seat (ask him) or record in parts named part 1, part 2. Don't rush to beat the clock.

If Loom won't install: on a Mac use QuickTime Player → File → New Screen Recording (pick your microphone); on Windows press Win + G for the Game Bar. Upload the file to the Drive folder instead of a link. Stuck more than a few minutes? Message Josh.
