Your Team

A new screen under Insights. One page, three parts.

Open Your Team

What it is

Your Team is the "how are we doing" page. It answers two questions on one screen: how is the campaign we're running going, and who on the team is doing the work. It's new, so there's no earlier version to compare it to — react to it fresh.

A reminder of one word: a touch is one logged contact — a call, a one-to-one email or note, or a handled reply — credited to the person who logged it. Bulk mail and email count toward the campaign, not a person (so no one "wins" just because a big mailing went out).

How it works. The top of the page is about the campaign: how the current one is performing, and a way to pull up a past one and compare. The bottom is about the people: who's made the most contact lately, and a full ranking of everyone. There's nothing to fill in here — it's a read-only scoreboard that updates from the work logged elsewhere (calls, touches, sends).

What this area should do for you

  • Tell you at a glance whether the current campaign is on pace or falling behind.
  • Let you compare it to a past campaign without hunting for numbers.
  • Show who's pulling the weight this month — and make it fair (a big mailing doesn't crown anyone).
  • Put per-person call stats in one place (they used to live on the Calls screen).

Part 1 · Current campaign

The big panel at the top left. It's the live scorecard for whatever campaign is running now — in the demo, "Loan maturity · Telegraph corridor," an MVI letter sent to 231 properties, still in the Calling stage.

It shows, in one place: how many pieces went out by mail and by email (and how many were delivered, returned, or bounced), how many replies and QR scans have come back, how many calls connected with an owner, how far through the call list the team is, whether the pace is ahead of or behind the weekly target, and how many appointments are set. Listings won is greyed — that arrives in a later phase.

What we want to hear from you

  • Is this the right set of numbers to know a campaign is working? What would you add or drop?
  • "Behind by 88" on pace — is that clear, and is it the number that would actually make you act?
  • Would you look at this daily, weekly, or only when something feels off?
  • If you had ten seconds on this panel, which number is the one you'd check first?

Part 2 · Past campaign performance

The smaller panel at the top right. Pick a past campaign from the dropdown and four numbers fill in — Sent, Calls made, Connects, Appointments — so you can compare it to the one running now. There's a View campaign link to open it in full. Before you pick anything it says "Pick a campaign to compare."

What we want to hear from you

  • Are those four numbers the right ones for a quick comparison? What else would you want lined up side by side?
  • Would you actually use this to compare, or do you judge a campaign on its own?
  • Is sitting the comparison right next to the current campaign helpful, or would you rather see a list of all past campaigns?

Part 3 · Team Performance

The bottom half of the page: who's doing the work. A time filter at the top right (Last 30 days / Last 60 days / All time) sets the window for most of this section.

Top performer (left). One person called out — in the demo, Marissa Chen, an ISA, with 412 touches in the last 30 days (3,186 all time), broken down into calls, connects, and appointments, and how far ahead of second place she is. This one is always pinned to the last 30 days, even if you change the filter, so it's a steady "who's hot right now." If no one has at least 10 touches it says "No leader yet."

Full Team (right). The whole roster ranked, with the top performer as #1. Columns: rank, name and role, touches in the window, and total touches — and you can sort by either touch column. ISAs and brokers are ranked together, not in separate lists.

What we want to hear from you

  • Is "touches" the right way to measure effort? The footnote explains what counts — does that feel fair to you?
  • Ranking ISAs and brokers in one list: right, or should they be separate?
  • Does pinning the top performer to 30 days (while the rest of the table follows the filter) make sense, or is it confusing?
  • Is a public ranking the right thing for this team, or does it create the wrong pressure? Be honest — this one matters.
  • The old Calls screen used to carry per-person stats. Now they live here. Is this where you'd look for them?

Scenario: Monday check-in

Optional. Talk out loud as you go. The data is fixed, so you're pretending.

It's Monday morning. The loan-maturity campaign has been running about four weeks. You want to know two things before your team call at 9: is this campaign working, and who should you recognize or nudge.

  1. Is the campaign on track? Look at Current campaign. In your own words, say how it's going. Is it ahead or behind? What's the one number that tells you?
  2. Behind on pace. It says "Behind by 88" with 412 calls this week against a 500 target. What would you do with that — push the team, extend the timeline, or something else? Did the screen make that obvious, or did you have to work it out?
  3. How does it stack up? In Past campaign performance, pull up "Just listed · 3310 Grand Ave" and compare. Is this campaign doing better or worse? Is four numbers enough to say?
  4. Who's carrying it? Look at Top performer and Full Team. Who would you call out on the team call? Is there anyone you'd want to check on?
  5. Change the window. Switch the filter from 30 days to All time. Watch what moves and what stays put (the top performer stays on 30 days on purpose). Did that behave the way you expected?
  6. Wrap up. After two minutes here, do you have what you need for the team call? If not, what's missing?
